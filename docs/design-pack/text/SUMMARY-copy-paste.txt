Falakus is an open, interactive web app that uses NISAR, the new NASA-ISRO radar mission, to show how Earth's surface changes and why. Optical satellites lose sight of the ground under cloud and at night, and about two-thirds of the planet is cloudy at any time. NISAR's L-band radar sees through both and revisits every place every 12 days.

We read calibrated NISAR data directly and compare its polarizations: HH reveals open water, HV reveals crops. A machine-learning model maps water on every pass. We tested it on dates it never saw, and NASA's ICESat-2 laser independently confirms the result (correlation 0.95). Radar phase shows ground motion after an earthquake, and ten years of satellite records drive a forecast of reservoir levels one month ahead.

Our case study is home. In Uzbekistan, 10.9 million people in the Fergana valley depend on reservoirs that cameras lost to cloud in 15 of 24 winter photos. We trace water released for cotton, the harvest field by field, and the Aral Sea's loss since 1985. Time-lapses, image sliders and plain-language briefings make each change clear to water managers, farmers and the public.
