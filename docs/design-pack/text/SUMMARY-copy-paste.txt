Falakus is an open, interactive web app that uses NISAR, the new NASA-ISRO radar mission, to show how Earth's surface changes, why, and what comes next. About two-thirds of Earth is cloudy at any time, so optical satellites often lose sight of the ground. NISAR's L-band radar sees through cloud and darkness every 12 days.

We built two machine-learning models. The first maps water from NISAR's HH and HV channels on every pass. Trained on clear-day Sentinel-2 photos and tested on dates it never saw, it beat the standard threshold method (map overlap 0.63 vs 0.59), and NASA's ICESat-2 laser independently confirms it (correlation 0.95). The second learned from ten years of monthly records and forecasts next month's reservoir size, 13 % more accurate than the best simple guess.

Our case study is home: 10.9 million people in Uzbekistan's Fergana valley depend on reservoirs that cameras lost to cloud in 15 of 24 winter photos. We show water released for cotton, harvests field by field in the HV channel, ground motion from radar phase, and the Aral Sea since 1985, through time-lapses, sliders and plain-language briefings for water managers, farmers and the public.
