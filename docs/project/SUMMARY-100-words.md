# Summary, ~100 words: 5 versions (see, compare over time, predict; whole world; honest). Pick one.

## 1 · See, compare, predict
Earth changes every day, but most change is hard to see: clouds cover two-thirds of the planet at any moment, and half of every day is dark. Falakus uses NISAR, the new NASA-ISRO radar satellite, to see through both, anywhere on Earth, every 12 days. Our machine-learning models find water, crops and ground motion in the radar, compare each place over time to measure how it changes, and forecast what comes next. Every number is checked against an independent source, and NASA's ICESat-2 laser confirms our water maps (correlation 0.95). Anyone can explore it, in plain language.

## 2 · A real disaster first
In May 2020 the Sardoba dam in Uzbekistan failed, and 70,000 people were evacuated. Changes like this build up out of sight, under cloud or at night. Falakus uses NISAR, the new NASA-ISRO radar satellite, which sees through both, to watch any place on Earth every 12 days. Machine-learning models turn the radar into maps of water, crops and ground motion, track how each place changes over time, and forecast the next month. A second NASA satellite, ICESat-2, confirms our water maps (correlation 0.95). The result is plain-language warnings anyone can read.

## 3 · For expert judges
Falakus is an open web app that turns NISAR L-band radar into time series of surface change. Calibrated HH and HV backscatter feeds machine-learning models that map water, crop growth and harvest, while radar phase shows centimetre ground motion. Each map is tested on dates the model never saw and checked against independent sensors: Sentinel-2 photos and NASA's ICESat-2 laser (correlation 0.95). Ten years of data drive a forecast that beats simple baselines 30 days ahead. Plain-language briefings let water managers, farmers and the public act on what radar sees and cameras miss.

## 4 · Short hooks
Cameras cannot see through cloud or darkness. Radar can. Falakus turns NISAR, the new NASA-ISRO radar satellite, into a clear view of a changing planet. It sees water rise and fall, fields grow and get harvested, and ground shift by centimetres, anywhere on Earth, every 12 days. Its models compare each place over time, measure the change, and forecast what comes next, checked against a second NASA satellite. We proved it on reservoirs that 10.9 million people in Uzbekistan depend on. Now anyone can see what was hidden.

## 5 · The invisible change
The most dangerous changes are the ones nobody sees: a reservoir draining under winter cloud, a flood at night, a hillside creeping by centimetres. Falakus makes them visible. It reads NISAR, the new NASA-ISRO radar satellite, which sees through cloud and darkness every 12 days, everywhere on Earth. Machine-learning models turn each pass into a map, compare it with every pass before it, and forecast the next month. Each result is checked against independent satellites and explained in plain words: what changed, why, and what comes next.
