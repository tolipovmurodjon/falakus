We built Falakus to track changes on Earth's surface that are easy to miss. Clouds cover about two-thirds of the planet at any time, so optical satellites often can't see what is happening below. NISAR, the new NASA-ISRO radar mission, can. It sees through cloud and at night and comes back to the same place every 12 days. We use its data to map water, crops and ground motion, follow how each site changes from pass to pass, and forecast reservoir levels a month ahead. Our water maps agree closely with NASA's ICESat-2 laser measurements (correlation 0.95). We tested it on reservoirs in our home country, Uzbekistan, but it works anywhere NISAR flies.
