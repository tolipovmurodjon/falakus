How to read it

Black: calm water. The surface acts as a mirror, so no echo returns.
Green: vegetation. Leaves scramble the wave (high HV).
Magenta: bare rough ground, rock, fresh rubble (high HH).
White: buildings (double bounce: ground → wall → satellite).

Colour = red & blue from HH, green from HV (NASA GIBS rendering of NISAR GCOV).

How Falakus works

NISAR→JPL GCOV→ASF DAAC→NASA GIBS→Falakus
1
The satellite measures.

NISAR (NASA + ISRO, launched 30 Jul 2025) carries an L-band radar (24 cm) and an S-band radar (~10 cm). It revisits every place every 12 days, ascending and descending.

2
JPL turns echoes into maps.

Raw echoes are focused, calibrated and terrain-flattened into GCOV: backscatter (γ⁰) per polarisation, HH and HV, 10–20 m pixels.

3
NASA publishes everything.

ASF DAAC archives every file (HDF5, ~2 GB each); NASA GIBS renders a daily global mosaic as map tiles.

4
Falakus finds your passes.

When you stop moving the map, we ask NASA's catalogue (CMR) which NISAR passes cover exactly your view, then stream only the visible tiles for those dates. Nothing is pre-downloaded; new passes appear as NASA publishes them.

5
We check radar against other sensors.

Optical (Sentinel-2/Landsat HLS) shows what a camera sees; Sentinel-1 shows C-band radar; NASA OPERA products give independent water and disturbance maps.

Early-warning signals, not prediction: radar can see slow ground creep before landslides, volcano swelling before eruptions, and soil saturation before floods. These are measurable precursors that cameras cannot see.

Datasets

How each change is detected

Water and floods

Calm water returns almost no echo (HH below ≈ −16 dB). New black area between two passes = new water. Flooded forest gets brighter: wave bounces water → trunk → satellite. Cross-check: OPERA DSWx-S1.

Wildfire scars and clearing

Burned or cut canopy loses volume scattering, so HV (green) drops by several dB. NISAR sees it through smoke. Cross-check: OPERA DIST-ALERT (optical vegetation loss) and EONET fire reports.

Landslides and quake damage

Fresh rubble is rough, so HH brightens; scoured slopes change texture. A pixel is flagged only when its change is much larger than its own normal pass-to-pass wobble.

Ground motion

Needs phase, not brightness: the GUNW interferogram product. One colour cycle = 11.9 cm of motion along the line of sight (half of 24 cm). This viewer shows backscatter (GCOV), so it shows surface damage, not centimetres of motion.

Honest limits

• GIBS tiles at zoom 11–13 are sometimes corrupted on NASA's side; Falakus detects them and shows the next-sharpest level instead.
• Provisional NISAR data before 21 Oct 2026; validated after. Instrument gap 27 Jul – 10 Aug 2026.
• Wind-roughened water can look like land; wet mud can look like water.
